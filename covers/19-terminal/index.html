$ stdout

NAME

stdout — every Vizuara cover is what the book's own code prints.

SYNOPSIS

$ vz cover <slug> --grid 120x80 --font "Martian Mono" --paper fanfold --ink carbon --signal selection

DESCRIPTION

Each cover is a printout. The top of the page is a field of real machine output — an attention matrix, a hex dump, a histogram of samples, a device mesh, a packed context window — arranged so that at arm's length it resolves into the book's one emblem, and up close every glyph is the actual number, token or line that makes it. This is Knowlton and Harmon's mosaic and the line-printer art of the 1960s, with the pictures made of the subject instead of symbols.

Below the printout the title is typed at a prompt. The prompt tells you the level; the block cursor at the end of the line is Vizuara's mark, on every book, in the one colour the system allows itself. One typeface, one character grid, three inks.

Where a subject has canonical data, the emblem is that data: GPT-2's own attention weights, MNIST's first test image with the softmax the book's program returns for it, Fisher's 150 iris flowers under the tree fitted to them. The rest are computed from their definitions: the H100 roofline from its published peak numbers, a GEMM's real products, the 80 links of a 5-cube.

PALETTE

Fanfold #F1EEE6
the paper
Carbon #17171C
ribbon ink; all tone is carbon
Selection #2B3BD9
the colour of selected text: the one datum that matters, the prompt, the cursor

Tone is made the way a line printer made it: with how much ink a glyph lays down. Ten steps mix carbon into paper (16–100%) while the variable weight climbs from 300 to 800; the last step is reverse video. No transparency, no halftone photographs; in greyscale Selection falls to a 35% grey that stays apart from Carbon.

TYPE

Martian Mono (Google Fonts, variable width + weight) and nothing else. Field: 10 px (7.5 pt), width 75, weight 300–800 as a tone control. Titles: 50 or 60 px, weight 800, set on 5× or 6× character cells. Subtitles 30 px. Imprint: width 112.5, weight 800, tracked.

GRID

120 columns × 80 rows; a cell is 6 × 11.1 px (1/16 in × 0.116 in at trim). Rows 0–50 are the printout and bleed on three sides; row 52 is a one-line caption — the expression that produced the picture; the title starts on row 56 with the prompt hanging in the margin; the imprint sits on row 76. All critical text sits at least 24 px inside the trim.

LEVELS

>beginner — a REPL prompt, the first thing you ever typed into $intermediate — a user shell #advanced — root coming soon — the cursor goes hollow, as it does in an unfocused terminal

The level is also spelled out beside the imprint, so nobody needs to know Unix to read it.

EMBLEM RULES

One silhouette per emblem, legible at 48 px, and no more than about 40% of the field left as even texture. A registry keeps neighbouring subjects from sharing a shape: triangle attention · glyph MNIST seven · bell samples · lattice 5-cube · window context · funnel compaction · partition decision tree · cross tiled GEMM · ramp roofline · grid ViT patches · waveform voice · bracket RL rollouts. Selection blue marks exactly one idea per cover. The cursor is one fixed block, 25 × 37 px, on the baseline of the last title line; subtitles follow as plain output.

SERIES

Charlie and the Intelligence Factory is one tmux session. A carbon status bar across the head of the printout lists the four rooms as windows; the current room is selected and starred, exactly as tmux marks the active window. On a shelf the blue tab steps left to right, I to IV.
