Specimen Plates

Machine learning, documented the way a nineteenth-century scientific atlas would have documented it.

Every Vizuara book becomes a numbered plate in one atlas: a single specimen of the book’s actual mechanism, engraved in line, lettered a, b, c and keyed in a legend at the foot, the way Gray or Haeckel documented theirs. The drawings are computed, not decorated: the descent paths are integrated on the real surface, the penteract is the real 25 device mesh, and the arches are real softmax weights.

The frame is strict so the specimens can be free: paper toned by level, one serif, a plate number, a scale bar, the title large at top left and the imprint small at the foot. Only two inks ever touch a figure, black that swells into shadow and paper-white that swells into light, so every plate prints as one spot tint plus black and still reads in greyscale and at thumbnail size.

No images, no animals, no people: the specimens are machines and mathematics, all vector, all generated by the page’s own script.

Titles are set at a text optical size (opsz 14, weight 600), so the Didone behaves like a sturdy Victorian scientific Modern with print-safe hairlines, not a fashion face. Legends and lettering are in italic. The plate apparatus is in letterspaced capitals.

Level: the paper, and a micrograph scale bar

Anatomy of a plate

  • Plate number: the book’s place in the catalogue, in roman numerals, top left.
  • Level: the paper colour plus a scale bar of one, two or three filled bars, top right.
  • Title: the hero, top left, broken by hand. Subtitles are italic, and a series line sits above.
  • Specimen: one mechanism, engraved and lettered. Occasionally a Fig. 2 gives its section.
  • Legend: the key to the letters, two lines of italic under a rule.
  • Imprint: the V-in-a-lens mark (two converging rays; viz, to see) and the name, small, at the foot.
