# Art-director review — 17-optical

**Score:** 7.5/10

**Verdict:** A strict, confident op-art system. The field-over-plate grid, the single Archivo family and the ink-plus-one-spot rule make the shelf read as one family, and most emblems are true to their subjects. The revision still has to fix a handful of real print slivers and edge collisions, drop one borrowed artwork, and make level-as-texture actually hold across the whole set.

**Strongest:** build-llms-from-scratch. The causal mask is the defining structure of a decoder-only LLM, and it is drawn exactly: attended cells are heavy horizontals, the masked future is thin verticals, and each token's own cell (the diagonal) is in violet. At 180px it reads instantly as a violet staircase. In greyscale the mask survives through the change from horizontal to vertical texture alone, so the form really does carry the concept. ai-context-engineering is a close second: its lens reads at thumbnail size and carries convincingly round the wrap.

**Weakest:** kernel-engineering. It is a near-verbatim restaging of Bridget Riley's Movement in Squares (1961), and NOTES names it as such. It is the only field in the set that is squares rather than lines, so it breaks the "one field of black lines" rule. The idea NOTES describes ("tiles compress toward the chip") isn't visible: there is no chip, only a yellow patch of eight checker squares. It is also the one cover that doesn't come from the designer's own vocabulary.

## Must fix

1. **charlie-language-room, charlie-vision-room, charlie-sound-room, charlie-reasoning-room** — The nearest wall line (z=1 in fRoom) is centred exactly on the trim. A 5px black bar is cut in half, leaving 2.5px of black running the full field height at x=0 and x=720 on all four covers. I measured it in the 2x PNGs: 5 device px of #121212 at both edges. With normal trim drift (about 1/32 in, roughly 2px) it will print as a random 0–5px sliver or vanish, and at 180px it reads as a frame line. It also breaks the NOTES claim that no line is cut into a sliver at the field edge.
   - *Fix:* In fRoom, start the wall-line loop at k=1, or offset the z sequence, so the outermost wall line sits at least 8px inside the trim, e.g. z = 1/(1+(k+0.5)*dz). If you keep an edge line at all, push it fully past the trim into bleed.
2. **transformers-from-scratch** — A stray 0.5px carmine hairline circle outlines the head disc (clearly visible at 2x around (216,236)→(412,40)). rings(B, R+p) draws a ring at r=198 with a 5.04px stroke, so it covers r 195.5–200.5. The clip at R=196 leaves a 0.52px sliver: sub-0.5px ink at trim that looks like an unintended outline.
   - *Fix:* Stop the coloured rings inside the clip, e.g. for (r=p/2; r + w/2 <= R; r+=p). Alternatively set R to fall mid-gap between rings (R=192, halfway between the rings at 186 and 198) so the clip never cuts a stroke.
3. **kernel-engineering** — The field is Riley's Movement in Squares: a checkerboard whose columns compress toward a vertical axis, and NOTES credits it by name. That is a recognisable, in-copyright artwork restaged on a commercial cover, which is a rights risk and against the brief's 'invent your own vocabulary'. It is also the only non-line field in the system, and the 'compress toward the chip' idea isn't legible.
   - *Fix:* Replace it with a line field that is the kernel engineer's own diagram: the roofline. Run horizontal lines at level pitch (8px, advanced), each displaced to follow a roofline profile: rising on the memory-bound slope, then flat at the compute ceiling. Put the ridge point (the arithmetic intensity where a kernel becomes compute-bound) in cadmium. It uses the same 'displace' grammar as the rest of the system and is exact to the subject.
4. **prompt-engineering** — At the emblem's focal point, the lowest teal lines cross and fuse into one solid slab (around x 330–390, y 250–262) with a lens-shaped paper sliver under 1px trapped inside it. The line above has a hairline gap. The ripple's slope (2πA/λ ≈ 0.94 with A=15, λ=100) compresses 16px pitch below the 8px line width, so the lines overlap. At full size it looks like a rendering glitch right where the eye lands.
   - *Fix:* Either lower the slope (A≈10, λ≈150 keeps the minimum local pitch around 9px, so gaps stay at 1px or more), or keep the black compression ring but clamp it. Compute each line's gap to its neighbour and, where the gap is under 1.5px, merge the two ribbons into one filled shape so no sliver is left. Check the teal disc again at 2x afterwards.
5. **sql-masterclass** — The coloured JOIN region is ragged on all four sides, so it reads as mis-registration. Red horizontals poke 4px past the outermost red verticals at x 264–268 and 452–456. Red verticals hang 12px below the last red row (y 364–376). At the top, red verticals start at y=184 and paint over the lower half of the full-width black row at y 180–188, so the black rule is half-covered by red ticks.
   - *Fix:* Snap the intersection rectangle I to the grating phase: I.x0/x1 on the outer edges of the first and last vertical (268 and 452), I.y0/y1 on the outer edges of the first and last row inside it. Use the same inclusive test (>=) for both the row and column loops, so every row or column is either fully ink or fully colour and each side of the red grid ends on a line.
6. **charlie-sound-room** — The first and last waveforms are sliced flat by the back-wall clip. The top line's peaks (around x 385–440) and the bottom line's troughs (around x 310–360) are cut straight against the frame. The stack starts at b.y0+14 and ends at b.y1−10, but the amplitude is ±14 plus half the stroke.
   - *Fix:* In rooms.sound, inset the stack by amplitude plus half the stroke plus the clip inset: for (y = b.y0 + 3 + 14 + p*0.25 + 4; y < b.y1 - 3 - 14 - p*0.25 - 4; y += p). Or scale the amplitude down on the first and last line, so every wave sits whole inside the frame the way the language lines and the vision iris do.
7. **mathematical-foundations-for-ml** — The caption and NOTES say the '1σ region' is inked in colour, but the mask is G > 0.32. Since exp(−r²/2σ²) = 0.32 gives r = 1.51σ, the vermilion area is the 1.5σ contour. That is a factual error in the text that will become the back-cover 'On the cover' note.
   - *Fix:* Either threshold at e^(−1/2) ≈ 0.607 for a true 1σ region (and raise s from 140 to about 210 if you want the red disc to stay the same size), or change the caption to 'the region within 1.5σ'.
8. **system (NOTES.md print spec)** — NOTES tells the printer that colour always overprints ink via /BM /Multiply. Only deit-from-scratch.pdf and transformers-from-scratch.pdf contain Multiply; the other 14 PDFs are knockouts. The 'minimum line or gap 1px, slivers filtered' claim also fails in several places: the 0.52px ring on transformers, the 0.32px paper slivers between blue and black stripes in DeiT's shifted patches, the sliver in prompt-engineering's teal slab, and the 0.77px gaps in the advanced grain mark.
   - *Fix:* Make the spec true. Either apply multiply/overprint to the colour paths in every field function, or reword NOTES to 'colour knocks out ink except on the two moiré covers'. In fDeiT, set the student stroke to p − w (+0.3 overlap) so shifted patches become a clean solid tone. Then add a check that rejects any gap or stroke under 1px, and actually run it.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **system** — Make level encoding true and legible. The 'shelf sorts itself by texture' claim fails on this shelf. neural-networks-from-scratch is beginner but the finest, silkiest texture (1.15px edges). pi-vs-hermes-vs-codex is advanced but has the coarsest stripes (about 30px pitch on the right). kernel-engineering is advanced with the coarsest squares, and the Charlie wall lines ignore level entirely. At 180px the caption-row mark is 3.5px squares, so level is invisible in the web library. Fix: make every field function derive its dominant pitch from GRAIN (NN stroke weight and neuron count, Pi's N and uncompressed pitch, Charlie wall spacing). Draw the grain mark with 3/4/6 bars instead of 3/5/8, so the advanced square's gaps stay at 1px or more instead of plugging to a grey block, as they already do on the presentation page.
2. **diffusion-lm-from-scratch** — Rethink the emblem. Each row is a fresh random layout, so no token is followed through the steps. That misrepresents masked diffusion, where positions are fixed and an unmasked token stays unmasked. It also averages to grey static at 180px, and the top half is sparse confetti. Instead, fix one token layout (one sequence across the 720px) and give each token an unmask step. Rows are denoising steps: a token is a violet [MASK] block until its step, then word-length ink from there down. The field becomes violet stalactites hanging from the top edge, each ending at a different depth. It is exact to the mechanism, it forms a bold figure, and it reads at thumbnail size and in greyscale.
3. **prompt-engineering** — Rethink the emblem. A pond ripple from a central point is generic ('small input, big effect'), and it radiates in every direction, so the 'downstream' in the caption isn't true. Keep the displaced-horizontal grammar but make it directional. The first ~110px of every line (left edge) is the teal prompt, each bent into a short specific shape (e.g. three repeated 'few-shot' bumps). The rest of each line, the completion, carries that same bend on to the right edge and slowly damps out. The prompt then visibly conditions every token after it, in reading order.
4. **neural-networks-from-scratch** — This is the one cover whose field doesn't bleed. There is a 22px paper strip above a 1.15px rule at y=22 and below y=522, so the gap to the caption row is 40px instead of the 18px on every other cover. At 180px the all-to-all mesh aliases into a grey mat, and in greyscale the viridian fan vanishes because it differs only in colour and a 2.4px stroke. Put the first and last neurons outside the field (y0 = −20, y1 = 564) so it bleeds top and bottom like the rest. Drop to about 6-9-9-6 neurons with edges of 2px or more so the fans become tonal wedges, and make the hot neuron's fan clearly heavier (4px). The same greyscale problem affects 5d-parallelism: the cadmium ribbon's tiles are the same form as their neighbours and fade to about 73% grey. Give that ribbon a form difference too, e.g. double stripe density or solid fills.
5. **system** — Plate rhythm and type details. The title block is anchored at the top, so two-line tails crowd the imprint: kernel-engineering and charlie-reasoning-room leave about 25–30px between the tail and VIZUARA BOOKS, against about 120px on 5d-parallelism. Cap tails at one line on the cover, or put hand breaks at the sense break: 'From silicon to speculative decoding —' / 'GPU kernels for modern LLMs' (and drop the lone full stop, since no other tail has one), and 'Reason — Reinforcement Learning,' / 'from bandits to reasoning models'. On deit-from-scratch, hang the opening parenthesis of '(DeiT) from Scratch' by about −0.3em so the D stem aligns with the B and I above. At −0.028em tracking the word spaces close up ('Pi vs', 'Foundations for'), so add word-spacing: 0.04em to h2.
6. **system** — Scaling to 50 books. Extend the planned ledger to track base geometry (horizontal / vertical / radial / grid / tiling / perspective) as well as field and ink. ai-context-engineering, mathematical-foundations-for-ml and prompt-engineering are already three 'horizontal stripes with a central swell' that look alike at 180px, and with 35 more titles from the same displace grammar that trope will dominate. On the Charlie covers, the series line in the foot is the same size and style of tracked caps, on the same baseline, as the imprint, so the row reads as one long run and competes with the signature. Move it to a kicker above the title (the unused .kick style is already defined) or into the caption row, and keep the foot for the imprint alone.
