Every Vizuara book is designed as if it had been pulled off a risograph: natural uncoated paper, two or three spot inks that overprint one another, halftone screens, and the slight misregistration of drums that never quite line up. The system is strict — one title face, one grid, the imprint always bottom-left, the level always carried by the key ink — and all the freedom lives in the emblem, which is always the book’s own mechanism drawn in ink. Where the inks cross is usually where the idea is: the overprint is the dot product, the inner join, the part of the context the model actually sees.

  1. Paper + drums. Every cover is one sheet of natural stock and 2–3 spot inks, printed in multiply. No gradients, no images: tone only comes from halftone dots and line screens.
  2. Key ink = level. Title, text and line-work print in one key ink, coded like a ski run: green circle, blue square, black diamond.
  3. Flash ink + emblem = subject. One or two bright inks per book, and one bold figure derived from what the book actually teaches.
  4. Fixed chrome. Level and length top; title top-left; the V press-mark bottom-left; the inks the cover is printed in, bottom-right.
  5. Special states. Forthcoming titles print as press proofs. Series share a building, a series ink and a numbered door.
