# Art-director review — 11-kolam

**Score:** 8/10

**Verdict:** A disciplined, conceptually rigorous system: real kolam rules, one dot per capsule (all 15 counts verified against books.json), a greyscale-safe level code (L* 92/33/10), identical type and imprint placement on every cover, and every title reads at 180px. It is let down by one visibly broken core-six emblem (5D), a fixed emblem box that crowds the footer on every cover, and too many plain-rectangle emblems for a 50-book shelf.

**Strongest:** ai-context-engineering: a two-strand braided frame (28 dots) around a separately woven 5×3 block (15) literally reads as "the window and what you put in it". The three-line count is honest and explained, the silhouette is unlike anything else on the shelf, and it reads clearly at 180px. The wrap's "How to read this cover" back panel is also a strong idea.

**Weakest:** 5d-parallelism: of the five "interweaving" lines, two are detached two-dot figure-8s hanging off the far left and right (the end dots of rows 4–5, around x≈185 and x≈535). A full-height column of turns around x≈310 seals a third line into an isolated 8-dot block. So three of the five parallelism dimensions cross nothing, which contradicts the caption "each weaving through the others". At full size and at 180px it reads as a rendering glitch, on a core-six book.

## Must fix

1. **5d-parallelism** — The annealer met 'exactly 5 loops' in a degenerate way. Two loops are isolated 2-dot figure-8 'ears' at the ends of rows 4–5, and a vertical wall of turns between columns 2 and 3 cuts off a third loop. Three of the five lines never cross another line. That misrepresents 5D parallelism, where every device sits in all five groupings, and contradicts the caption.
   - *Fix:* Add solution constraints for this book in books.js/kolam.js: reject any trace where a loop covers fewer than 6 dots, and reject any trace where some pair of loops never crosses (check pairwise crossings from the traced loops). Force 'x' on the horizontal edges that join the row-4/5 end dots to their neighbours, and forbid a turn on every vertical edge of any single column seam. Re-run for loops:5. If MY symmetry can't satisfy this, drop to sym:'none' with the same constraints.
2. **kernel-engineering** — The caption '32 pulli · the line has begun' and the system rule 'one pulli per capsule' (stated again on the back cover) together assert that the book has 32 capsules. books.json has no capsule count for it, so the cover states an invented fact.
   - *Fix:* Keep the 8×4 warp but drop the number from the caption: '8 × 4 · one warp · the line has begun' (and the Tamil equivalent). Or draw the not-yet-real dots as open rings so they read as provisional. Regenerate with the real count once it is known.
3. **ai-context-engineering (wrap)** — Print hazard on the spine. The whole 43-dot kolam is squeezed into about 34×24px with 1.6px lines and sub-pixel gaps, and it already closes up into a solid cream block on screen. On press it will be a blob. The spine also has 1px grey border-left/right rules that would print as lines on the hinge.
   - *Fix:* On the spine, use a legible excerpt: one row of the frame braid at ≥2.5px stroke with ≥2px gaps, or just the imprint mark and the pips. Remove the .spine borders from the printable artwork and put fold marks outside the trim or on a separate guide layer.
4. **ai-context-engineering (wrap)** — The Tamil at the start of the back-cover spec line ('43 புள்ளி · மூன்று கோடுகள்') is a synthesized oblique. It inherits font-style:italic from .back-spec, and Anek Tamil has no italic, so Chrome fakes a slant.
   - *Fix:* Add `.back-spec [lang=ta]{font-style:normal}` (or `font-synthesis:none` on [lang=ta]). Keep the italic for the Latin part only.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **system** — The fixed emblem box sits too low. On every cover the kolam ends at y≈790 and the footer text starts at y≈813, a constant 23px gap, while the gap between title and emblem runs from 64px (DSA/DeiT/5D) to 244px (R Masterclass). The emblem looks jammed onto the imprint and caption. Move the box centre from (360,596) to (360,574), so the box spans y 376–772. That gives ≥40px to the footer and still ≥40px below the deepest title block, which ends at y=336.
2. **system** — Silhouettes need a budget before this scales to 50. 8 of 15 emblems are plain rectangles or rectangles with a notch (ACE, LLMs, Pi, DeiT, Vision, Kernel, Language, Prompt), 4 are diamonds or octagons (MFML, NN, 5D, DSA), and 2 are stepped pyramids (R, Reasoning). At 180px on the same kaavi ground, LLMs (4×5) and Vision Room (5×4) are near-twins, and Pi and Vision Room rely on the same no-turn/gcd lattice trick. Add a silhouette class and a shelf check in build.js (hull aspect ratio plus fill ratio, and reject near-duplicates within a level). Reserve plain rectangles for subjects that really are grids (patches, a warp). For LLMs, try a tall 2×10 tower of five 2×2 blocks joined by single forced crossings: the classic N× transformer stack, and a silhouette nothing else on the shelf has.
3. **deit-from-scratch** — Rethink this emblem. 'Two lines = class token and distillation token' is a stretch, and the plain patch grid repeats Vision Room's metaphor. Make it true to DeiT: a 8×5 patch grid (40) plus two token dots, [CLS] and [DIST], added one pitch above the top-left corner of the grid, for 42 dots. Force crossings where the token dots join the grid, so the two token lines visibly thread into the patches. That gives a distinct 'tab' silhouette and an accurate caption.
4. **dsa-in-python** — The 9×9 octagon has nothing to do with the subject. Its stated reason ('an Euler tour') is true of every kolam in the set. Try a hash table with chaining instead: a 9-dot bucket column on the left with chains running right of lengths such as 9-4-8-2-9-6-9-4-9 (=60), for 69 dots. Each chain is a forced-crossing braid (a linked list), and the line passes between chains only through the bucket column. The pitch is 44, so it fits the box at about 440×396, and it makes a ragged comb silhouette that nothing else has.
5. **r-masterclass** — The 1–2–4–7–8–7–4–2–1 columns, with a single-dot finial, read as a stepped pyramid, Christmas tree or temple tower (the notes rule out temple motifs), not as a bell curve. They also duplicate Charlie IV's pyramid silhouette. Use a bell with long tails and a flat top: 1–1–3–5–8–8–5–3–1–1 (=36, 10 columns). The thin tails running out along the baseline are the cue that says 'normal distribution'. Separately, Charlie IV is the only emblem whose line runs orthogonally (idukku) rather than at 45°. Either keep idukku as a declared series-only exception, or say so in the system notes.
6. **system** — Craft fixes. (1) Re-break deit-from-scratch to three lines at 64px: 'Build a Data-Efficient / Image Transformer / (DeiT) from Scratch' (about 603/548/568px, which fits the 620px measure). This keeps the book's key term unsplit and reduces the title from four lines to three. (2) Charlie IV subtitle: break after 'Learning,' so the first line doesn't end on a dangling 'to': 'Reason — Reinforcement Learning, / from bandits to reasoning models'. (3) Drop the trailing period on the Kernel Engineering subtitle so it matches the other subtitles. (4) Double-line crossings are drawn as merged X-junctions for self-crossings, and in paint order (one strand always on top) between different loops. Charlie IV mixes both. Render crossings as consistent over/under, or consistently flat, across all four Charlie books.
