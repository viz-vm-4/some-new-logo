# Art-director review — 12-blockprint

**Score:** 8/10

**Verdict:** Chhaap is a rigorous, unmistakably ownable system: a fixed kinar and pallu, a crisp Eczar title, level shown by the dye bath, and one carved block per book. It holds as a family at 180px, and all the text, margins, fonts, counts and levels are clean. What needs fixing is in the emblems: one real rendering bug in the Reasoning Room, a "5D" block that shows only three dimensions, and an accent layer that nearly disappears on madder and indigo grounds and in greyscale.

**Strongest:** charlie-vision-room: this is the one cover that reads instantly at 180px. It has a single large silhouette, and the colours map attention weight directly (anar > kora > indigo > centre thread only), so the mechanism itself is the image. The ikat feathering makes it read as cloth rather than a heatmap. On the main list, build-llms-from-scratch does the same job: the causal mask stamped straight becomes a bold sawtooth that is correct and legible at thumbnail size.

**Weakest:** ai-context-engineering: of the core six this block says the least about its subject. At 180px it reads as a generic framed grid, much like a QR tile. The four "streams" are drawn in only two forms (dots N/S, dashes E/W), so instructions, tools, retrieval and memory can't be told apart. The lozenges at the nodes stand for nothing. The indigo "query" cell is a 10px dark square that barely shows. You could put this block on almost any matrix book in the catalogue.

## Must fix

1. **charlie-reasoning-room** — Every up-pointing policy chevron is drawn pointing down. In IKAT.reasoning, rot() at index.html:448 maps 'u' to o[y][x]=m[4-x][y]. Because the '>' chevron is vertically symmetric, that gives exactly the same output as 'd'. In the policy, 9 of the 70 free cells should point up. In the render, the two cells directly below the top-right goal, (7,2) and (7,3), point away from the goal and into the wall at (7,4). The right-hand column (9,4) to (9,7) and cells (2,6), (2,7), (6,7) are also reversed. The caption says 'computed by breadth-first search', so the cover shows a wrong optimal policy.
   - *Fix:* Change the 'u' branch to `sx=4-y; sy=x` (o[y][x]=m[x][4-y]), which gives the rows 00000/00100/01110/11011/10001, a '^'. Re-render with --only charlie-reasoning-room and check that every chevron in the column under each yellow goal points toward it.
2. **5d-parallelism** — The book is '5D' and your own back-cover blurb on the wrap lists five axes: data, tensor, pipeline, sequence, experts. The block encodes three of them: a GPipe schedule for pipeline, stages split in two for tensor, and the repeat for data. Sequence/context and expert parallelism are missing, and the 'About the cover' note right under the blurb admits as much. On a core-six book whose title is the number of dimensions, this misrepresents the concept.
   - *Fix:* Keep the GPipe schedule, which is correct: 4 stages, 3 micro-batches, backward from t=9-s. Encode the missing two axes as well. (1) Sequence: split each micro-batch cell into left and right halves as well as the existing top and bottom, so each cell becomes a 2x2 of 3.2x5px sub-cells. (2) Expert: make one stage an MoE stage, e.g. stage 2, where each forward cell prints only one of its four sub-cells, in a position that changes per micro-batch (token routing). Update the figcaption, NOTES and wrap colophon to name all five axes.
3. **cnn-fundamentals** — The caption calls the block a Hinton diagram, but the squares are not scaled to weight. Sides are 27/21/14px for |w|=16/2/1. In a Hinton diagram, area is proportional to |w|, and because a LoG kernel sums to zero, the negative ring should carry the same ink as the positive centre. Here the black ring carries about 4.6x the ink of the white centre (4x21^2 + 8x14^2 = 3332 vs 27^2 = 729). The render therefore reads as a dark surround with a small white dot, which inverts the kernel's actual weighting.
   - *Fix:* Set side = 27*sqrt(|w|/16) (centre 27px, -2 cells about 9.5px, -1 cells about 6.8px; all still well above print minimums). This gives a truer and more striking buti, a big reserved square with a fine dark halo. If you want to keep the current look, stop calling it a Hinton diagram and describe it as a 'weight map' in the caption and NOTES.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **system** — The accent layer carries meaning on half the covers, but it is indigo on madder or madder on indigo, about 1.9:1 contrast, and in greyscale it goes from 78 to 41 luma. In my greyscale proof, the 5d-parallelism backward pass, the build-llms diagonal, the transformers look-back arcs and the pi-vs-hermes summary cells all merge into the ground. Borrow Ajrakh's own resist outline: give every accent-layer shape on a dyed ground a 1.5–2px kora keyline (reserve), or print a kora underlay 1.5px larger before the accent pass. Then run a greyscale contact sheet as a standing check, aiming for at least 3:1 between the accent and the ground.
2. **ai-context-engineering** — Rethink the block as 'the packed window'. Make it one long context-window frame (e.g. 240x60, brick repeat) with a fixed budget, packed left to right with segments from the four sources, each in its own carved texture: solid bar = system instructions, stripes = tool output, dots = retrieved chunks, small chevrons = memory. Put the query as a madder cell at the right end, and cut the last segment off at the frame edge to show the budget being enforced. Mirror the packing on alternate stamps. The subject is choosing what fits into a fixed budget, and a course of bricks also gives the cover a silhouette unlike the grid-and-check covers.
3. **pi-vs-hermes-vs-codex** — The three lanes are the same sawtooth shifted in phase, so the 'vs' is missing and at 180px it reads as a repeating bar chart. Keep the lanes unattributed, since you're right not to make claims about the products, but give them three canonical strategies with visibly different rhythms. (a) Summarise and reset: the current sawtooth. (b) Accumulating memory: the madder summary base steps up one cell after each compaction. (c) Rolling window: the stack plateaus and holds a flat top. The caption can say 'three compaction strategies' without saying which agent uses which. The subtitle names memory, and this makes memory visible.
4. **system** — Anchor the field repeat to the kinar, not to the top trim. At the moment oy=0 (index.html:535), so the kinar cuts whatever row lands on it: a 17px sliver of window tops on ai-context-engineering, stubs of bars on pi-vs-hermes, half cells on 5d and build-llms. That looks accidental next to the clean decision-trees field. Set oy = KINAR_Y - ceil(FIELD_H/BH)*BH so a whole block row sits on the border and the partial row falls at the top edge, where trim and bleed take it. That is also how a printer works, starting from the border.
5. **system** — Add a scale ladder to the planned block grammar. Every main-list block is stamped at a 120–192px module, 4–6 repeats across. At 180px, 7 of the 11 printed fields read as fine small-cell check or dot cloth (ai-context, build-llms, 5d, pi, diffusion, cnn, kernel), and across 50 books that register will get monotonous. The Charlie covers show what scale does. Let a block be stamped at 1x, 2x or 3x (6, 3 or 2 repeats across), chosen per book so its main silhouette is at least ~25px in the thumbnail. For example, stamp the cnn kernel and the diffusion 8x8 at 2x, and allow single-motif fields for mechanisms with one strong shape.
6. **build-llms-from-scratch** — The current break, 'Build Large Language / Models (LLMs) / from Scratch', splits the core noun phrase. Use 'Build Large / Language Models / (LLMs) from Scratch'. The widest line is then about 590px, so it still sets at the 64px cap, with a clean rag that grows line by line. On 5d-parallelism, 'for Large Model / Training' strands 'Training' and splits 'model training'. '5D Parallelism for / Large Model Training' sets on two lines at about 62px and matches the '...for' line end you already use on mathematical-foundations-for-ml.
