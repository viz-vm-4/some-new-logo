Chhaapछाप — the impression a carved block leaves on cloth

Every Vizuara book is a length of hand-block-printed cotton. Each title gets its own carved block, built from the actual mechanism of its subject — a causal mask, a pipeline schedule, a Sobel kernel — and that block is stamped across the cloth as a repeat, the way an Ajrakh printer in Kutch builds a field from a single carved unit. The stamping is itself the idea: a block repeated with a stride across a surface is a convolution, and the same carved unit printed again and again is a model replicated across data.

The structure comes from the textile, not its ornaments: a field of repeats, a black kinar border shared by every book, and a pallu end-panel reserved from the dye where the title is set. Level is the number of dye baths the cloth has been through: raw kora for beginner, madder for intermediate, indigo for advanced. The four Charlie books are woven, not printed: a double-ikat sub-series after the Patola of Patan, where every thread is dyed to a pre-computed grid — a bitmap made of thread.

Type — both from Indian foundries

Eczar Bold, for titles Vaibhav Singh / Rosetta. A sharp, calligraphic display serif designed alongside its Devanagari. Anek Latin, for the imprint Ek Type, Mumbai. Variable width; used wide and tracked for the stamp line.

The shelf
