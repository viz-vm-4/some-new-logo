MuraqqaEvery book is a folio from one album.

A Mughal muraqqa — the imperial album of the Delhi, Agra and Lahore ateliers — gives every painting the same architecture: a coloured margin (hashiya), a stack of ruled borders in gold, colour and black (jadval), a cartouche for the words, and at the centre a small, flat, exact picture.

Vizuara Books borrows that page whole and changes only what is inside it. The margin tells you the level; the cartouche carries the title in large type; the miniature is a jewel-toned, diagrammatic painting of the book's actual mechanism — the attention mask, the device mesh, the join — painted with the flat grounds of Basohli and the tipped-up rooms of Rajput painting. The imprint is a shamsa, the rosette that named an album's owner.

Grounds — one per subject family

The miniature and cartouche share one flat ground, chosen by the book's subject family and never from the level pigments. A forthcoming book is shown on primed, unpainted wasli; it gets its family ground when it is published.

The core set

Six folios. The page architecture never moves; the painting and its ground change with the book.

Range

A short title and a very long one; a forthcoming book, shown as an unfinished miniature (underdrawing complete, only the blocks already computed are painted); and the four-book Charlie and the Intelligence Factory as a sub-series — one pavilion, four rooms, each with its mechanism on the back wall and the floor.

Print wrap

Back · spine · front for Neural Networks from Scratch at trim (7.5 × 9.25 in, 0.5 in spine). The verso is the facing page of the folio: plain wasli inside the same borders, with a colophon that explains the painting. On the spine the cartouche turns on its side.

What was taken, and from where

Structure from the Mughal album folio (Jahangir- and Shah Jahan-period muraqqa, c. 1605–1658): painting inside a jadval of gold, colour and black rules, inside a contrasting hashiya; text in a lobed cartouche; the owner's name in a shamsa. Colour logic from Pahari painting at Basohli (Jammu hills, late 17th century): one flat, saturated ground per picture, no modelling, no sky-gradient. Space from Rajput court painting (Mewar, Bundi, Kota): the pavilion drawn frontally with its floor tipped up in plan — the "rooms" of the Charlie series. Process from unfinished miniatures, where the sanguine underdrawing on primed paper shows beside the first laid colour.

Nothing figurative, nothing sacred, no paisley. The only "picture" the system ever shows is a small garden of cypresses and a pool, used as the input image in the vision books.

For a production version

Commission a working miniature painter from the living Pahari or Rajasthani ateliers (Kangra, Chamba, Jaipur, Udaipur, Kishangarh) to rule a master jadval and paint a master shamsa by hand, paid per edition and credited by name on every colophon ("Borders ruled and seal painted by …"). Print the gold as a metallic spot or foil, the grounds as spot colours; the flat grounds are chosen to survive greyscale — the three margins step light, middle, dark.
