InterchangeEvery book is a map of its own territory.

Each Vizuara book is drawn as a transit diagram of its subject. The book’s parts are the lines, every capsule is a station — one tick, one capsule, counted from the live library, so a 69-capsule book visibly runs longer than a 9-capsule one — and the subject’s own architecture is the geography: a transformer’s residual stream, a tree’s splits, the five axes of a GPU cluster.

One strict frame holds fifty different maps together, the way Beck’s grammar holds a city together: a signage-grade title that is always the loudest thing, a key that doubles as the table of contents, and the imprint as a quiet signature. Level is read from across the room — beginner and intermediate books are day maps, advanced books are night maps, and a trail grade (● ■ ◆) repeats it in print.

Map legend — the same on every cover

Linea part of the book; colour follows part order, so every book sets off on the red line.
Tickone capsule. Counted, not drawn for effect: every map is audited in code against the catalogue.
Ringa capsule where lines meet — where one part hands you to the next.
Pillan interchange across a bundle: a layer, a gate, the capstone.
Bulletthe part number at a terminus; it matches the key.
Territorythe subject’s geography, in tint, named in italic the way maps name rivers and hills.
Hollowa line under construction: the coming-soon cover.
