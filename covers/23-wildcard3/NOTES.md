# Blazon — the Vizuara Roll of Arms

**Every Vizuara book is granted arms.**

## 1. Thinking first: concepts considered

Taken directions (do not touch): Swiss grid, engraved plates, Pelican modernism, engineering drawing,
generative, giant glyph, riso, foil cloth, transit map, model kit x2, kolam, block print, Mughal,
Bombay signs, Jantar Mantar, visual puns, op art, 3D objects, ASCII x2, cut paper.
Also banned: circuit boards, node-link decoration, robots, brains, bulbs, blueprints, glow gradients.

I looked for vocabularies far from technology that are (a) a *formal system* with rules, so 50+
books come out as one family, and (b) able to carry the real mechanism of each book.

### Why heraldry wins

- **It is already a programming language.** A blazon ("Per bend grady of eight Argent and Gules")
  is a terse formal description that any herald can render into exactly one picture. Printing the
  blazon next to the arms is the heraldic equivalent of showing the code next to the output —
  exactly the spirit of a library of "from scratch" books.
- **The mechanisms fit the grammar, not just the look.** Heraldic divisions are recursive
  axis-aligned cuts (a decision tree *is* a blazon). The causal mask is a line "grady" (stepped).
  Heraldic *marshalling* — quartering, impalement, escutcheons of pretence — is literally how you
  compose things: parallelism is quartering, an SQL join is impalement, knowledge distillation is a
  teacher's arms borne *in pretence* on the student's shield.
- **It was designed for the 180px problem.** Arms were made to be recognised across a battlefield:
  flat tinctures, a hard outline, few charges, and the *rule of tincture* (never metal on metal,
  colour on colour) — a contrast rule from the 1300s.
- **It was designed for print.** Petra Sancta hatching (1638) is a complete system for printing
  every tincture in one ink; the covers survive greyscale by construction.
- **It is a system that scales to thousands**, with families (the Charlie books share a chief and
  are differenced by cadency marks, the way brothers were), and a lexicon where each charge keeps
  one meaning across the whole library.
- **Nobody else is near it.** It is not engraving (02: flat colour, no hatching on the covers), not
  foil or cloth (08), not a Mughal album (13), not a visual pun (16: the charge vocabulary is
  structural, not a one-off gag), not a grid (01).

Risk I took seriously: heraldry can slide into Ye Olde pastiche (Gothic lettering, mantling, lions).
So the drawing follows *modern civic heraldry* — the reduced, two- and three-tincture municipal
arms of mid-century Finland and Switzerland — with no helms, mantling, supporters or blackletter.
Only the shield, the tinctures, the blazon.


## 2. The direction

**Name:** Blazon — the Vizuara Roll of Arms.

**The idea.** Each book is granted a coat of arms whose divisions and charges are the mechanism the
book teaches, drawn in flat heraldic tinctures with a hard sable outline. Beside the shield, where a
programmer would expect source code, is its *blazon*: the terse formal sentence that heraldry has
used for eight centuries to specify exactly one picture ("Per bend grady of eight Argent and
Gules" *is* the causal mask). Under it, a one-paragraph *symbolism*, as in a real grant of arms.
Every cover is laid out identically: imprint and roll number on top, a big left-set title, the
shield at the right in the same box on every book, the register (blazon, symbolism, level,
extent) in a column at the left. The shelf reads as one armorial; each shield reads as one book.

## 3. The system

**Grid (720 × 888 px trim).** 44 px margins. Imprint row at y 34. Title block from y 86, 632 px
measure. Shield 400 × 480 at x 276, y 340, always. Register column 206 px wide at x 44, y 348–820,
level/extent pinned to its foot on a 2 px rule.

**Level = the ground the shield is displayed on** (lightness steps survive greyscale):
- Beginner — **Argent** ground (paper) `#ECE6D9`, sable type
- Intermediate — **Or** ground `#D4A236`, sable type
- Advanced — **Sable** ground `#161517`, argent type, and the shield fimbriated (rimmed) Or
- The level is also written at the foot of the register.

**Field of study = the tincture of the shield's field:**

| Tincture | Hex | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Argent | `#F8F5EE` (ground `#ECE6D9`) | metal; beginner ground |
| Or | `#D4A236` | metal; intermediate ground; most charges |
| Gules | `#B3261E` | language — LLMs, transformers, prompting, finetuning |
| Azure | `#213F93` | foundations — maths, classical ML, code and data |
| Vert | `#1D6946` | vision and embodiment — CNNs, ViTs, VLMs, robots |
| Purpure | `#5E2B5F` | agents, context and memory; RL |
| Sable | `#161517` | compute and systems — parallelism, kernels, serving; advanced ground |

The old orange / sky-blue / magenta pinwheel is gone entirely. All shields obey the rule of
tincture where it matters for legibility, and every division is outlined in sable, so each shield
reads in greyscale and prints in one ink via Petra Sancta hatching (shown on the page).

**Type.** Newsreader (Production Type, Google Fonts), one family through its optical-size axis:
Semibold at opsz 72 for titles (86 / 70 / 58 px for 2 / 3 / 4 lines, shrunk only if a line would
overflow; 76 px when a series line or subtitle is present), italic text sizes for blazons,
letter-spaced semibold capitals for imprint and labels. Titles are broken by sense by hand.

**Imprint.** Vizuara's own arms: *Sable, a chevron reversed Or* — the chevron is heraldry's
builder's charge (a roof's rafters), reversed it is a V. Used as a one-colour mark beside
VIZUARA BOOKS, smaller than any title. Each book carries its number in the roll (Nº 01–52, the
catalogue order in books.json).

**The Ordinary (lexicon).** One meaning per charge across the whole library, so the language is
learnable: roundel = token / data point; annulet = the token still to come, or a neighbourhood;
billet = a document / a prompt; mullet = the query; estoile = a reward; lozenge = another class;
bend = a boundary drawn by a line; grady (stepped) = causality; fess = the context window;
pall = a branch; chequy = a matrix or an image; canton = a kernel; barry = the turns of a
conversation; plain = a summary; escutcheon = a whole system (in pretence: a teacher);
quartering / lines of partition = work shared out; impalement = a join; pallets couped = a
waveform; chief sawtoothed = the Intelligence Factory.

**Sub-series.** The four *Charlie and the Intelligence Factory* books share a chief sawtoothed Or
(the factory's north-light roof) and are differenced on it by the English marks of cadency —
label, crescent, mullet, martlet for first to fourth son — plus a series line above the title.

**Coming soon.** Unpublished books are shown *in trick*: the heraldic draughtsman's outline with
the tinctures written in (sa, or, gu). Drafted, not yet painted; painted on publication.
