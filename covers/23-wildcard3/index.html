Vizuara Books · a cover system

Blazon

Every Vizuara book is granted arms. Its shield is drawn from the mechanism it teaches, in heraldry’s own grammar, and printed beside its blazon — the eight-hundred-year-old formal language that compiles to exactly one picture.

The idea

Heraldry is the oldest identity system that works at a distance: flat tinctures, a hard outline, a few bold charges, and a rule of contrast (never metal on metal) that is seven centuries older than WCAG. It was built to tell a thousand houses apart across a field. Fifty books across a library grid is an easier job.

And its grammar is uncannily computational. Heraldic divisions are recursive axis-aligned cuts, so a decision tree is a blazon. A line grady (stepped) is the causal mask. Marshalling is composition: parallelism is quartering, a SQL join is impalement, and a distilled student bears its teacher’s arms in pretence.

The drawing follows mid-century civic heraldry — the reduced municipal arms of Finland and Switzerland — not Gothic pastiche: no helms, lions or blackletter. One shield, placed the same way on every cover, the blazon set beside it like source code beside its output, and a quiet publisher’s mark.

Tinctures — the field of the shield names the field of study

Each small square is the tincture’s Petra Sancta hatching (1638), heraldry’s own system for printing every colour in one ink. The shields can be reproduced in black alone for a one-colour spine, a stamp or a fax-grade printout and still be read correctly, and the covers’ three grounds are light, middle and dark in greyscale.

The Ordinary — one meaning per charge, across the whole library

An ordinary of arms is the heraldic reference that indexes shields by what is on them. This is Vizuara’s: every new book is blazoned from the same vocabulary, so a reader who has learnt that a pile is a context window can read the next shield unaided.

Type

Newsreader, Semibold at display size

and its italic, at text size, for the blazon: Per bend grady of eight Argent and Gules.

One family, Production Type’s Newsreader, used through its optical-size axis: the 72pt master for titles (tight, sharp, high contrast), the text masters for the register and letter-spaced capitals for the imprint. It is a reading face for a publisher of reading; nothing on the cover is set in a “tech” font.

Titles are set left, broken by sense, never leaving a short word alone, and sized in three steps (two, three or four lines) so a shelf of fifty keeps one voice.

The Roll — sixteen books

A family of rooms — the Charlie books share a chief and are differenced like brothers

All four Charlie and the Intelligence Factory shields carry the Factory’s sawtoothed chief — its north-light roof — over the room’s own device. On the chief sits the heraldic mark of cadency that distinguished a first, second, third and fourth son: the label, crescent, mullet and martlet. Book numbers you can see at a glance, in a language that already existed.
