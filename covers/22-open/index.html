Vizuara Books · a cover system · direction 22

Textmode

Every Vizuara book teaches a machine that reads and writes text, so every cover is made of text. Each figure is set in a monospaced font on a strict 120 × 74 character grid, the way the first computer art came off line printers. It always shows the book’s real mechanism, with real numbers in it: the causal mask, the device mesh, the gradient step. No picture of “AI”.

The title, set large in Fraunces, is the hero and ends in a text cursor. The Vizuara cell mark sits small in the corner as a signature. Hue files a book on its shelf, and lightness says how deep it goes, so the level survives a greyscale printer and reads across a room.

Everything on the covers is live HTML, SVG and type: no raster images, nothing generated by an image model. Every glyph is placed on the grid by code, so a cover prints as sharp as the text inside the book.

01 · Grid120 × 74 cells

A cover is a terminal screen of 6 × 12 px cells. Figures are drawn only with glyphs, slope-aware lines (- / | \), frames (+--+) and inverse-video tiles that carry a number or a label. Margins are 6 columns and 3 rows.

02 · Title firstFraunces, then a cursor

Titles are set by hand (no orphans) and sized to fill 648 px, up to 112 px. Text after a colon becomes the italic subtitle. The last line ends in a cursor block in the second ink: the shelf signature.

03 · HueHue is the shelf

Eight subject families, as Penguin once coded its genres by colour. Each family is exactly three inks (paper, vivid, deep), so any cover can be printed in three spot colours.

04 · LevelLightness is the level

Beginner books are printed on the family’s paper, intermediate on its vivid, advanced on its deep. The other two inks draw the figure. The level stays visible in greyscale and at thumbnail size, and the meter in the corner spells it out.

APaletteEight families × three inks, defined in OKLCH so each level's ground sits at nearly the same lightness in every family (paper L .95, vivid L .60–.71, deep L .26–.30). That is what makes level a lightness.

The same monospace draws the figures, the imprint, the level, the caption and the lesson count. Only glyphs from the Latin, Latin-1, punctuation and Greek sets are used, so every cover renders from the Google Fonts subset alone.

The Vizuara cell

One character cell with a V knocked out. It is the text cursor at rest, the same block that ends every title. It works as a favicon, as a spine mark and as a blind-deboss, and it replaces the retired pinwheel without borrowing any of its colours.
