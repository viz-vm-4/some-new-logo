One grid, one typeface, three inks — and on every cover a single figure, constructed from the idea the book teaches.

01 Rationale

Every cover is set on the same 12 × 15 module grid, in one typeface, like a Müller-Brockmann concert series or a Gerstner programme: the system is the brand, so the Vizuara name can afford to be quiet.

The ground carries the level — paper for beginner, signal red for intermediate, ink black for advanced — so fifty spines read as a gradient of difficulty, even photocopied in grey. On that ground stands exactly one figure, drawn from the book’s own mechanism: the causal mask of a GPT, the device mesh of 5D parallelism, the direction field of an ODE. Whatever is set in the third ink is the one thing the book teaches you to see.

What it is not: no illustration, no mascots, no gradients, no “AI” clip-art. Every mark is a square, a circle or a bar placed on the grid in SVG, so the covers are vector from thumbnail to billboard, and every figure can be explained in one sentence on the back cover.

02 The programme — six rules

  1. 1Grid12 × 15 square modules of 44 px with 12 px gutters and 30 px margins. 720 × 888 divides exactly. Every element — type, figure, mark, rule — sits on it.
  2. 2TypeArchivo, nothing else. Bold for the title, flush left from the top-left corner, as large as the longest line allows: 68, 60, 50 or 46 px. Regular for data. Lines are broken by hand; no widows.
  3. 3InksPaper, Ink and Signal Red. The level decides which ink plays ground, which plays figure, and which is the signal.
  4. 4FigureOne per book, built from squares, circles and bars on the module grid, standing on the colophon rule or bleeding off the edge. It is the mechanism of the book, not a picture of its topic. A grid of module squares is reserved for mechanisms that are matrices (attention, a device mesh, tiled GEMM, image patches); anything that is merely tabulated finds its own silhouette.
  5. 5MarkThe ribbon: a bookmark hanging from the top edge in column 12, its tail notched into a V. Its length is the level — one, two or three modules. On the spine it hangs the same way.
  6. 6ColophonBelow a 4 px rule, three cells: imprint, level, length. Series books add a fourth: the book number over the series name. Nothing else is printed on the front.

04 Typeface

Archivo
Bold 700

Archivo (Omnibus-Type, Google Fonts) is a grotesque cut from the nineteenth-century Akzidenz tradition the Swiss worked in — sturdy, slightly condensed capitals, a closed a and g — with a variable width axis in reserve for a longer catalogue.

Title Bold 68/64, 60/57, 50/48 or 46/45 px, tracking −2.4 %, trimmed to cap height so the first line sits exactly on the top margin.
Subtitle Regular 21/26.
Colophon Bold + Regular 13.5/17.
Figure labels Semibold 13.

05 The grid, the mark

Title
Rows 1–4, columns 1–11. Cap height of the first line on the top margin. Title sizes step down until the longest hand-broken line fits eleven columns.
Figure field
Rows 5–14, all twelve columns, bleeding off the left, right or top edge when the idea continues beyond the page (a history, an eigenvector, a corridor). Figures stand on the colophon rule.
Ribbon
Column 12, hanging from the trim. Level 1 = one module, Level 2 = two, Level 3 = three. The swallowtail notch cuts a V.
Colophon
Row 15, below a 4 px rule. Cells start on columns 1, 4, 7 and 10.

08 Sub-series — Charlie and the Intelligence Factory
