Direction 18  ·  A cover system for Vizuara Books

Maquette

Every book is one object, made in code and photographed on seamless paper.

A maquette is the small model a sculptor makes before the real work, and every book in this library is about a model. So each cover is a single sculpted form that embodies its subject: a saddle surface with its straight rulings for the mathematics, a stone window packed block by block for context engineering, the shadow of a five-dimensional cube for 5D parallelism.

The system is a museum catalogue. The title is set large on the paper wall. The object sits in studio light. At the foot is a gallery label giving the object's name, its material, and its dimensions, measured in capsules and hours.

Nothing is drawn or stock. Each cover is ray-marched in the browser from signed-distance functions (soft shadows, occlusion, studio softboxes) straight onto a 2250 × 2775 px canvas, 300 dpi at trim.

Level is lighting. Beginner books are shot bright and airy, advanced books under a single spot on dark paper. It is a difference of value, not hue, so it survives greyscale print and reads from across a room. A small grey-scale target, the kind photographers put beside an artwork, repeats it in words. Every render checks its own wall value against its key (±8 levels).

The label

Object
What the sculpture is, in plain words: Cube with a mouth. Column of twelve identical blocks.
Medium
Plaster, terracotta, travertine, oxblood glaze, beeswax, marble. The colour on the shelf comes from the material, never from a brand accent.
Dimensions
The book's true size: capsules × hours. A forthcoming title reads Forthcoming, and its object is shown unfinished: raw plaster, the steel armature still showing, graphite pointing marks, a paper tag tied on.

Imprint

A V standing on a plinth: the publisher as the one who puts ideas on display. It is always small, always top left, and never louder than the title.

Range

Short title · long title · forthcoming

The same rules stretch from a two-word title to a three-line one. A forthcoming book gets the sculptor's working maquette of its object: raw plaster, steel armature, pointing marks and a tied tag. The catalogue can announce a book before its cover is "cast".

Charlie and the Intelligence Factory

Sub-series · I–IV

Four rooms, one cube. The sub-series keeps a single object — a porcelain cube, the "room" — and changes only the one opening cut into its face: a mouth for Language, sixteen patch-windows for Vision (Transformers), a stepped ear for Sound, a six-step stair for Reasoning. The cuts are gilded inside, a nod to the golden ticket. The paper still follows each book's level.

Print wrap

Back · spine · front, one continuous photograph

The studio keeps going around the spine. The back cover is the same sheet of paper, lit from the same softbox, and it carries the catalogue entry for the object on the front.

How it is made

One WebGL2 fragment shader shared by every book: an analytic paper sweep (floor, cove, wall), a sphere-traced object, soft shadows toward a softbox, ambient occlusion, a studio environment of softbox, scrim and cards for reflections, a gentle filmic shoulder and photographic grain. Each book supplies one distance function and one material function, about thirty lines of GLSL. If WebGL is unavailable the covers fall back to flat paper in the level colour, with all the typography intact.
