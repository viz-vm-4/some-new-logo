# Art-director review — 18-sculpture

**Score:** 8/10

**Verdict:** A distinctive, well-made museum-catalogue system: every title reads at 180px, the paper key shows the level at a glance even in greyscale, and the PDFs carry the objects as 300dpi rasters with vector type underneath. It is let down by several gallery labels and captions that don't match the objects shown, one widowed word, an intermediate paper that renders too light, and three emblems that read as the wrong thing (Prompt, Neural Networks, Charlie Reasoning and Vision).

**Strongest:** ai-context-engineering. The "context window" is packed block by block, with headroom at the top and one block left out on the floor. It is true to the subject, a little witty, reads immediately at 180px, and the mixed stones give the most colour in the set without any brand accent. It shows the whole idea (one object, one studio, one label) at its best. Runner-up: 5d-parallelism. The rhombic icosahedron really is the zonohedron of five icosahedral axes, the projection of a 5-cube, so the caption's "five parallel directions" is literally true.

**Weakest:** prompt-engineering. A near-textureless dark slab tipped by a few degrees looks like a closed laptop or phone on a riser. The "small input, large change" lever idea isn't visible because the lift is tiny. The "slate" looks like matte plastic. In greyscale the red-lacquer wedge (about luminance 64) merges with the slab's underside (about 80), so the one idea-carrying element disappears.

## Must fix

1. **kernel-engineering** — Tagline widow: "From silicon to speculative decoding — GPU kernels for modern / LLMs." leaves "LLMs." alone on the second line (confirmed at 2x). The brief explicitly forbids orphaning a single short word.
   - *Fix:* Add `text-wrap: balance` (or `pretty`) to `.tag` so it breaks as "From silicon to speculative decoding — / GPU kernels for modern LLMs.". Also add a non-breaking space before the last word of every tag as a system rule, so the Harness and Inference Engineering taglines can't widow either.
2. **deit-from-scratch** — The label says "led by a distillation token", but the gilt token stands at the tail of the row: rightmost, nearest the camera and last in reading order. The model also leaves out the class token, which DeiT's input sequence has as well as the distillation token ([CLS, DIST, patches]). The oblique camera (yaw -50) hides the patch faces, so "one image as sixteen patches" can't be read. In greyscale the iron-oxide disc (about the same luminance as the limestone) disappears.
   - *Fix:* Put the tokens at the head of the row. Add a plain class-token slab (unglazed plaster, same height as the gilt one) next to the gilt distillation token at the left end, and change the label to "…led by a class token and a distillation token". Turn the camera to about -25° so the patch faces show the disc. Darken the ink to a near-black oxide (about #3A1A12) so the disc survives greyscale.
3. **build-llms-from-scratch** — The label says "Stoneware, oxblood glaze", but the lit faces render coral or salmon (sampled RGB 207,109,98), not oxblood. The shadow faces are crushed to pure black (RGB 6,0,0). In a catalogue-label system the medium line has to match the object shown.
   - *Fix:* Lower the exposure or albedo so the lit faces land around #7A2320 to #8E2B25 with a glossy highlight, and add a bounce card on the right so the shadow faces sit at about 15–25% instead of 0%. Alternatively, change the label to "Stoneware, red glaze".
4. **ai-context-engineering** — The front label lists "Travertine, slate, marble, terracotta", but the render also has sandstone (the pale yellow blocks) and serpentine (the green block, per the shader). The back-cover copy on the wrap lists sandstone, so the front and back of the same book disagree.
   - *Fix:* Make the medium line "Travertine, slate, marble, sandstone, terracotta, serpentine". If that runs too long, replace the serpentine block with slate and use "Travertine, slate, marble, sandstone, terracotta" on both front and back.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **system** — The intermediate paper renders far lighter than specified, and unevenly. Sampled wall values on intermediate covers range from about 165 to 215 (Build LLMs is the lightest, about 200–214). The specified stone is #A8A39A (about 164), chalk renders about 240 and soot about 55. So beginner vs intermediate is the weakest distinction on the shelf and in greyscale, and it is the level signal the system depends on. Retune the k1 lighting preset (lower boxLum or exposure, or darken the paper to about #9A958C) so the wall at title height lands at 150–160 on every intermediate cover. Add a check to the render step that samples the wall at a fixed point per key and fails if it drifts more than ±8.
2. **system** — White currently means both finished and forthcoming. The forthcoming rule uses white plaster, but Math (plaster), 5D (Carrara), all four Charlie cubes (porcelain) and DeiT (limestone) are white or near-white too. So Kernel Engineering doesn't read as forthcoming anywhere except the word in its label, and 8 of 14 objects are pale boxes or solids on grey paper, which will look repetitive at 50 books. Make the forthcoming state visible on the object itself: raw plaster with a visible steel armature, pointing marks and a tied paper tag. Add a material rule for finished books (for example, at most about 1 in 4 white objects per paper key, with a spread of warm, dark and metallic materials).
3. **charlie-reasoning-room** — Series fix. (1) Reasoning: the five black recesses with thin gold risers read as a bar chart or phone signal-strength icon, not a stair, and the gilt treads are almost invisible. Cut a real flight of 6–7 steps that recedes diagonally into the cube face, lit from top-left, so the horizontal gilt treads catch light and the risers stay dark. (2) charlie-vision-room and charlie-sound-room are near-twins at 180px: a white cube with a round gilt hole on stone paper. Give Vision a 4×4 grid of small square apertures, which is an eye made of patches and fits its subtitle "Vision Transformers". Keep the round stepped horn for Sound only, and re-render the horn to remove the jagged, broken ring edges at its lower right.
4. **prompt-engineering** — Rethink the emblem, keeping the system's materials: a small carved stone cylinder seal (the prompt) resting at the start of a long clay slab, rolled once to leave a continuous impressed frieze (the model's continuation). It reads immediately as a small input shaping a long output, it is true to how prompting conditions generation, and it gives the chalk shelf a new silhouette. If the wedge idea is kept instead, make the lift obvious (slab angle of 15° or more), give the slate a cleft texture, and switch the wedge to a value that contrasts in greyscale (for example, pale gilt or unglazed chalk-white).
5. **neural-networks-from-scratch** — This is a core-six book, and the 4→4→1 ball-and-stick in a square 3D layout reads as a chemistry molecule model, the most generic image in the set. The rods also carry dark shadow smears at the crossings, and the 'terracotta' has a satin sheen that reads as copper. Alternative that fits the system: a string construction in the tradition of Gabo and Hepworth. Three upright oak boards are the layers, brass pins are the neurons, and taut linen threads run between every pin on adjacent boards, with thread thickness as weight. It is fully connected, clearly layered and clearly sculpture. At minimum, lay the layers out as a planar frieze facing the camera, make the material matte (rough about 0.9, no clear coat), and add fill so rod shadows don't go to black.
6. **build-llms-from-scratch** — The column's top (y≈265) sits level with the italic '(LLMs) from Scratch' line. To avoid it, the column has been pushed to x≈418–505, making it the only off-centre object in the system. Keep twelve modules but scale them to about 0.8 so the top clears the subtitle by at least 40px, then centre the column on x=360 like every other object. A smaller point: '(LLMs)' belongs with the roman title, not the italic 'from Scratch' line. Consider 62px (the s2 size) with '(LLMs)' ending the second roman line.
