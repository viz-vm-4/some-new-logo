# Art-director review — 16-wit

**Score:** 7.5/10

**Verdict:** Double Take is a witty, disciplined system. The shelf reads as one family, every title holds at 180px, and most emblems meet the designer's own "technically true" rule. It is held back by a few drawing-level slips (a derailed train, a colliding token, a 5-cube that shows 31 GPUs), two greyscale failures, and three emblems (RL, Kernel, Pi) that don't yet meet that rule.

**Strongest:** ai-context-engineering. Bruner and Minturn's B/13 figure, read as A·B·C across and 12·13·14 down, is a real double take made only from type. What the figure means changes with its neighbours, and that is the discipline the book teaches. It reads instantly at 180px, it's true, and nobody else's list would have it. Runners-up: rag-in-production (the retriever pun is instant and accurate) and charlie-vision-room (the patch grid becomes a raster-order queue, which I checked and is correct).

**Weakest:** reinforcement-learning. "Carrot on a stick" is the most worn-out incentive image in business slides, the same reason the designer killed the iceberg. The caption says "reward and penalty on one pole", but the drawing has no penalty: the stick is just a pole. The idiom also means a reward that is kept forever out of reach, which is the opposite of how reinforcement learning works. There is no agent and no loop. Charlie IV (the bandit) makes the same RL point far better on the same shelf.

## Must fix

1. **5d-parallelism** — The caption says the 5-cube is 'exactly … 32 GPUs', but this symmetric projection puts vertices 01010 and 10101 on the same point (360,300). I computed it: only 31 distinct GPU squares are drawn.
   - *Fix:* Stretch the vertical (data, k=0) axis by 1.25: multiply dirs[0] by 1.25. I tested it. The centre pair moves 33px apart, the closest two nodes are then 33px apart, the bounding box stays at y 70–530 (clear of the key at 572), and every family of edges stays parallel.
2. **5d-parallelism** — In greyscale the key falls apart. By L*, tensor (yellow, 77) is close to context (blue 50%, 67), and pipeline (red, 50) is close to expert (grey, 57). I rendered it in greyscale and four of the five key swatches become near-identical greys.
   - *Fix:* Draw each key swatch at its axis's real angle (−90°, −54°, −18°, 18°, 54°) and keep the colour as well. Direction then carries the mapping, which is the cover's own point about families of parallel lines, and the key survives greyscale.
3. **build-llms-from-scratch** — The caption and NOTES claim 'a tower of identical tiers, as a decoder is a stack of identical layers', but the tiers taper from 520px to 160px. A narrowing stack suggests a bottleneck architecture, so the picture contradicts its own technical claim. Also, the Arabic ع and ب hang out of their windows: the tail of ع and the dot of ب drop onto the sill.
   - *Fix:* Make all seven tiers the same width (for example 300px with 6 windows each) and have the crane lower an identical 8th block. If the ziggurat silhouette must stay, drop the 'identical layers' claim. Swap the Arabic glyphs for letters without descenders (ه, ط, ك), or centre each glyph on its bounding box instead of its baseline.
4. **kernel-engineering** — The idea gets the CUDA model backwards. The caption says 'a cob is a grid of kernels. One of them is the one you're writing.' In CUDA you write one kernel and launch it over a grid of thread blocks, and every cell runs that same kernel. The red kernel implies the other cells are different kernels.
   - *Fix:* At minimum, rewrite the caption so red means one block of your kernel ('every kernel on this cob runs your code; the red one is the block you're profiling'). A better fix is to rethink the emblem; see improvements.
5. **pretraining-mini-kimi-k3** — The train is a straight side view placed on an elliptical track, so it is visibly derailed. The last (paper) car's wheels float about 35px below and outside the outer rail where the track curves up, and the red cowcatcher on the left hangs off the rails.
   - *Fix:* Either shorten the train to engine plus two cars inside x≈200–520, where the near rail is almost flat, or place each car on the ellipse and rotate it to the local tangent so every wheel sits on the near rail.
6. **charlie-language-room** — The half-emerged next token (x 418–452) overlaps the '␣the' token (x 332–424), so they fuse into one wide yellow block with its label off-centre, and the 'next token coming out of the slot' idea is lost. Separately, Albert Sans has no ␣ glyph, so the PDF embeds DejaVuSans as a fallback. That glyph is heavier than the rest and sits below the baseline.
   - *Fix:* Move the four tokens about 24px left (or narrow them) to leave a 14px gap. Draw the emerging token behind the red machine so it comes out of the slot (for example x 438–470, with the machine painted over its right half). Draw the space marker as a small SVG ⎵ bracket in ink, about 3px thick, instead of using U+2423.
7. **decision-trees-from-scratch** — The leaves are 'coloured by predicted class', but red (L* 50) and blue (L* 35) print as two similar mid-greys. In my greyscale render the two classes are indistinguishable, so the technical point disappears in greyscale.
   - *Fix:* Encode class twice, the same way level is encoded. Give one class a second, shape-based signal: for example a 3px paper midrib down the blue leaves, or a rounder leaf shape for one class. Keep hue as the first signal.
8. **build-llms-from-scratch** — Shapes that are meant to touch leave anti-aliasing seams. There is a pinkish hairline at every Babel tier joint (visible in the 1x JPG as 223,215,196 against 240,229,211). The same problem appears on mathematical-foundations-for-ml: light lines between the three temple steps (66 against 25 on black) and under every Π lintel. They show at full size on the web, and some PDF viewers and RIPs render them as hairline gaps.
   - *Fix:* Overlap shapes that meet by 1px, or merge each tier stack, step stack and Π (lintel, stems, brackets, feet) into a single path.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **reinforcement-learning** — Rethink the emblem. Use Skinner's pigeon instead: the word 'reinforcement' comes from operant conditioning. One flat pigeon pecks a lit yellow key and a single grain drops from a hopper: agent, action and reward in three shapes, and it's accurate. Let the pigeon's neck and body carry the diagonal the pole currently gives the composition, so the cover keeps its energy.
2. **system** — The title block needs a rule for long text. I put a three-line title with a two-line subtitle into your own CSS and the subtitle runs into the imprint, because the 612px hang line is fixed. Anchor the text block from the bottom (last line a fixed distance above the foot) and let the picture zone shrink, or set hang lines by line count. Use the h2.s 50px size, which is already in the CSS but unused, for rare four-line titles. Also write down the line-break rule and apply it: 'Pretraining a / Mini Kimi K3' → 'Pretraining / a Mini Kimi K3'; all four Charlie titles 'Charlie and the / X Room' → 'Charlie and / the X Room' (I tested it at 56px and it fits); 'Build Decision / Trees from Scratch' → 'Build Decision Trees / from Scratch' (also fits and keeps the noun phrase together).
3. **kernel-engineering** — The corn pun works only on the word, which is the test you used to kill the 'match being struck' idea. Try a waffle iron instead: one press (the kernel) closes over a grid of identical square cells (the launch grid, and the tiling that kernel work is really about), and every cell cooks at once. One red cell is a single thread block. It's accurate, it's a clean flat silhouette on black, and it rhymes with the grid better than the cob does.
4. **pi-vs-hermes-vs-codex** — The drawing is beautiful, but at 180px it reads as a poetry or botany book: nothing shows the flower was flattened to fit. Keep the picture and make the compaction visible. Make the stem too tall for the page and fold it back on itself in one sharp crease near the top edge, and splay the petals flat so they overlap at the edges. The flower has then been squashed to fit a fixed page, which is the context window. Keep the mirrored tint imprint as memory; it's the best detail on the cover.
5. **python-for-data-science** — The bulge is a rounded rectangle with a paper frame, so it reads as a tablet or monitor showing a spreadsheet, not a swallowed table. Also, the column lines disappear in the solid paper header row. Build the swelling from the body's own outline (44px → about 160px → 44px with smooth tangents, like Saint-Exupéry's drawing), let the table's four corners push small angular bumps into the skin, and drop the frame line. The same 'reads as a chart' problem affects charlie-sound-room: bars standing on the belt look like a bar chart. Mirror them about a centre line above the belt so they read as a waveform.
6. **inference-engineering** — The hand reads as an L-shaped white pipe or periscope ending in a plug. There are about 170px of white forearm before the cuff, and the thumb path is completely hidden behind the tray and the 60px cloche. Put the cuff right at the wrist (about 40–50px of glove below the palm, then the cuff, then the black sleeve that vanishes into the ground). Bring the thumb up over the tray's front edge and add a knuckle notch, so the figure–ground trick lands and the shape reads as a hand.
