Direction 15 · Vizuara Books

Jantar

Instruments in stone: a cover system
for Vizuara Books

In Delhi and Jaipur in the 1720s and 30s, Sawai Jai Singh II built mathematics at the scale of architecture: a right triangle 27 metres high that tells the time by its shadow, a bowl that maps the sky, and marble quadrants marked finely enough to read the time to a few seconds. Every Vizuara cover is one such instrument, drawn in axonometric and derived from the book's own subject: a stepwell whose terraces are the layers of a neural network, a wall whose single window is the context window, a court split by ever-lower walls that is a decision tree.

The drawings are generated in code by a small sun-and-stone renderer: each structure is modelled, lit by one hard sun and given true cast shadows, then output as flat-toned vector polygons. The system itself is simple. The title sits in the sky, the instrument stands on the ground, and a section cut through the earth forms the plinth that carries the imprint. The time of day gives the level: morning for beginner, noon for intermediate and evening for advanced.

The three hours

Level is carried by the sky (the one flat field that is both sky and ground) and by the light itself: morning is pale and even, noon is ochre and high, and evening is indigo with a raking sun and long shadows. The sky value steps from light to mid to dark, so the level still reads when printed in greyscale. A small stepped glyph in the plinth (one, two or three steps down) repeats it in the text.

Type

Anek Latin, SemiBold, width 80
Titles, imprint and labels. Designed by Ek Type (Mumbai). A condensed width keeps long titles on two or three lines at monumental sizes.
Tiro Devanagari Hindi, Italic
Subtitles, captions and the back cover. The Latin of Tiro Typeworks' Devanagari family, a bookish counter-voice to the stone.

Anatomy

Sky

Title top left in ink or limewash, 80 px for two lines and 68 px for three. The only colour field, it changes with the level.

Instrument

One structure per book, modelled from its subject, drawn in axonometric, lit by one sun and cast in true shadow. The materials are red sandstone, white marble edging and coursed masonry.

Section line

At 748 px on every cover the ground is cut open. Pits and wells show in section, and the earth is drawn as poché in oxblood.

Plinth

Holds the imprint: a stepwell mark (an inverted stepped V), Vizuara Books, the level glyph, capsules and hours, and a one-line figure caption naming the source.

The shelf

13 covers · 720 × 888 px · 7.5 × 9.25 in trim

Print wrap

Back · spine · front, the drawing continuous across all three

Sources

Jantar Mantar, Jaipur (Sawai Jai Singh II, 1728–34; UNESCO World Heritage): the Samrat Yantra, whose gnomon is inclined at the site's latitude of about 27°, and the Jai Prakash Yantra. Jantar Mantar, New Delhi (1724): the Misra Yantra and the free-standing stairs. Chand Baori, Abhaneri (9th century), with its criss-cross flights of steps. Panna Meena ka Kund, Amer. Jali screens of Rajasthan and Gujarat. These buildings supply structure, light and material; nothing is traced from them.

A production version would commission an architectural illustrator to check each instrument against measured drawings (for example the survey drawings of the Jaipur observatory) and would credit the sandstone and lime-plaster craftspeople whose finishes the palette is taken from.
