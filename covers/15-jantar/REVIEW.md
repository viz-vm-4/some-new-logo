# Art-director review — 15-jantar

**Score:** 7.5/10

**Verdict:** Jantar is an original system that is unmistakably Vizuara's own and holds together as a shelf: a fixed stage, level carried by the time of day, and an oxblood section plinth. All data is correct (core six present, capsule and hour counts match books.json, fonts embedded, no retired palette, no logos). Before it ships, the revision needs to fix Neural Networks (a muddy drawing that crowds the imprint), a level glyph that looks like tofu, 5D Parallelism's emblem (it shows only data parallelism) and the illegible Pi vs Hermes vs Codex drawing.

**Strongest:** build-llms-from-scratch. The stair ends in a dashed setting-out block, the step that hasn't been built yet. That is next-token generation, stated truthfully in the system's own vocabulary. It reads instantly at 180px (a clean diagonal silhouette against ochre), the render is crisp with a lit tread and marble nosing on every step, and the caption earns its place. A close second is transformers-from-scratch: the Jai Prakash bowl is the most memorable silhouette on the shelf, and the curved shadows of the crosswires meeting at one mark are physically right.

**Weakest:** neural-networks-from-scratch. It is the only cover drawn flat and frontal on a peach panel that stops 20px from both trims. At full size the terraces break into noisy fragments of flight in near-identical shade reds (#8A3124/#99392B), with scattered pale specks that look like render noise, so the terraces don't read as layers and the flights don't read as connections. The pit also cuts through the plinth down to y=806, leaving the imprint jammed underneath. It is a core-six title and currently the least legible drawing in the set.

## Must fix

1. **neural-networks-from-scratch** — The pit and water section runs down to y≈806, and the imprint row (mark plus VIZUARA BOOKS / BEGINNER) starts at y≈811–813. That leaves 5–7px of clearance, against ~65px on every other cover, so the imprint looks jammed under the drawing. The pit's peach back panel also stops 20px from the left and right trims: that is neither the 40px stage margin nor a full bleed, so it will look like a trimming error.
   - *Fix:* Keep the band intact on this cover. End the section cut at y≤778 (≥30px above the r1 cap line), or raise the well so its water surface sits at the plinth line PL=748 with only a shallow stepped notch cut into the plinth. Then either bring the pit edges in to the 40px margin or bleed them fully off both trims.
2. **all covers (level glyph)** — The level glyph beside BEGINNER / INTERMEDIATE / ADVANCED is one small filled rect plus two 1.2px outlined rects. At 1x and in print it reads as '▀□□', the .notdef tofu boxes a failed font produces. Anyone proofing these will report a font fallback. It also has no visual relation to the stepwell imprint mark.
   - *Fix:* Remove the outlines. Draw all three steps solid, with the steps not yet reached in a dim plinth tone (e.g. #8C4A3E on #5B1D18) and the reached steps in #F4EADC. Better still, make it the mark's own stepped-well profile with 1/2/3 terraces filled. Enlarge it to about 30×18 with ≥3px gaps so it survives print and doesn't read as a glyph box.
3. **5d-parallelism** — The emblem and caption misrepresent the subject. A 5×5 field of identical gnomons, captioned 'One instrument, replicated', shows only data parallelism in two dimensions. 5D parallelism is specifically about the four axes that split one model (tensor, pipeline, sequence/context, expert) on top of replication. As drawn, the cover says 'data parallelism'.
   - *Fix:* Keep the field and the evening shadows, but split each instrument. Cut every gnomon crosswise into 3–4 stage segments with ~6px gaps along the hypotenuse (pipeline). Split each one lengthwise into two thin leaves (tensor). Let one row carry differently-shaped instruments (experts). Replicate the rows (data). Change the caption to something like 'One instrument, split five ways'.
4. **pi-vs-hermes-vs-codex** — Two of the three folds don't read. The switchback renders as a notched block with a sliver-thin gap and no visible landings. On the spiral, the rear treads show as disconnected floating wedges (left of the column, about x 560–640, y 360–470), and the front treads are separated by strips of sky, so it reads as a turbine or totem, not a stair. The straight flight is cropped by the left trim, which hides the equal-height comparison. Also, left-to-right order plus 'less and less ground' (NOTES) implies a ranking in which Codex compacts best. That is an unsupported claim about three real products.
   - *Fix:* Make the spiral a continuous helix: treads overlapping 30–40% in plan with no sky gaps, and the column carried to the top landing. Give the switchback clear half-landings and put the sun on its treads so each flight shows a lit zigzag. Bring all three structures inside the 40px margin, standing on one ground line with equal top heights. To stay neutral, give them roughly equal footprints (three different folds, not a ranking), and caption it 'Three ways to fold one climb' without the compactness order.

## Improvements (highest impact first)

1. **system** — The system's value structure fails in greyscale on two of the three hours. On evening covers the plinth #5B1D18 against the indigo sky #1E2046 is 1.21:1, so the section line (the system's signature) disappears in greyscale on 5d-parallelism, pi-vs-hermes-vs-codex and kernel-engineering, and the Charlie I/IV poché frame melts into the sky. On morning covers, lit stone #F8D2B6 against limewash #EFE6D7 is 1.14:1, so lit faces dissolve (mathematical-foundations quadrants, the decision-trees tall wall), and decision-trees' marble-class leaf blocks (#FFFBF4 on #EFE6D7) are effectively invisible, which hides half the 'two classes' idea. Fix: draw a 2–3px marble cut line (#FFFBF4 / #F4EADC) along the section top at y=748 on every cover. It is also good sciagraphy and sharpens the 'cut in section' idea. Put decision-trees' second class in shade stone or oxblood rather than marble.
2. **charlie-* (sub-series)** — The frontal elevation makes all four rooms read as a facade or a garage door with windows. The actual mechanism (a pierced roof, with the sun drawing its pattern on the back wall) is invisible: the roof holes show as smeared pale stripes on Vision and Sound. At 180px, Sound and Vision can be told apart only by a ~40px pattern. Tiny stray ground-line ticks sit outside the frame at x≈172 and x≈548, y≈670 on all four. Vision's holes are 44×26, so the 'ViT patches' come out as letterboxes, not square patches. Fix: use the system's axonometric camera (az ~25°, el 24°) with the near wall cut away in poché, so the pierced roof slab and the light shafts are seen. Make the Vision holes square. Delete the ticks.
3. **mathematical-foundations-for-ml** — The camera at az 14° shows the gnomon almost end-on, so the right triangle (the whole point of 'the right triangle and the circle', and the image on the wrap's front) reads as a thin blade. The right quadrant is cropped at the trim, leaving an odd 15px dark sliver at x 705–720. Rotate to about az 40° so the triangular profile and the quadrant's quarter-circle both read. Then either keep the right quadrant inside the 40px margin or crop it decisively (≥60px off the page).
4. **ai-context-engineering** — The 'four lit tokens' read as three and a half: only their top and right faces catch the light, the fourth is half-lit, and the window's light patch lands in front of the row instead of on it. Lit and unlit tokens are also close in value (pale marble versus marble shade), so at 180px nothing is lit. Move the token row, or the window, so that exactly four tokens stand fully inside the patch. Put the unlit tokens in stone shade (#8A3124) so the four marble-white lit ones pop at thumbnail size.
5. **system** — Stairs already appear in 5 of 13 covers (build-llms, pi-vs-hermes' three stairs, charlie-reasoning, the NN stepwell flights, the math gnomon's stair). Across 50 books, 'stair = progress' will get dull and blur the emblems together. Cap stairs at about 1 in 8 and draw on the real Jantar vocabulary: Ram Yantra (an open cylinder with radial pillars), Rashivalaya (twelve gnomons each tilted for a different sign, a natural fit for mixture-of-experts titles such as the Kimi books), Digamsha (a pillar inside two concentric walls, for retrieval/RAG), Nadivalaya (a two-faced equatorial dial, for encoder–decoder). With only three sky colours, silhouette has to carry distinctness at 180px.
6. **kernel-engineering / charlie-reasoning-room** — Subtitle rag: 'From silicon to speculative decoding — GPU kernels for / modern LLMs.' and 'Reason — Reinforcement Learning, from bandits to / reasoning models' both break mid-phrase and leave a short dangling line. Break at the natural pause: 'From silicon to speculative decoding — / GPU kernels for modern LLMs' (and drop the trailing period, which no other subtitle has), and 'Reason — Reinforcement Learning, / from bandits to reasoning models'. Or set text-wrap: balance on .sub.
